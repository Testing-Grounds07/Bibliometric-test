    How the combined score works

    Every co-citing article contributes one point, plus a diminishing bonus for the largest repeatedly co-cited bundle it supports for this pair. A pair-only article contributes 1; a triple contributes 2; a ten-reference bundle contributes about 4.17. Overlapping bundles do not multiply an article’s contribution. This is an experimental score, not a significance test. Click a line for its score, original article count, and support at each order. Larger bundles are not automatically more topically specific.

    Method & interpretation

    One co-citation network, multiple orders

    Choose combined orders, pairwise, or a higher-order bundle size. Every pair or bundle requires five distinct supporting articles. Reference scopes retain works cited at least 10, 20, or 30 times; the largest available order depends on scope.

    The combined score sums 1 + log₂(K − 1) across distinct co-citing articles, where K is the largest qualifying bundle containing the pair. Each article contributes once: a pair-only article contributes 1; a triple contributes 2. Overlapping bundles do not multiply an article’s contribution. This experimental score is not a significance measure or a validated bibliometric standard.

    Choose the network model separately from the algorithm. Pairwise and higher-order retain the existing citation scores. In Hypergraph mode, whole groups become article–group incidence networks. Leiden uses bipartite CPM (cross-class gamma; no within-class penalty). Infomap models weighted flow through both articles and groups, encoding both types of visit. This preserves group membership but is memoryless, not a multilayer memory model or direct hypergraph modularity. The separate experimental direct modularity optimizer remains available. Pair-line intensity still shows citation evidence; envelopes show whole groups. Hypergraph incidence has its own CPM scale and settings/reliability checks; the ordinary Auto CPM audit is not reused. Display filters do not recalculate communities; distances are not measured similarities.

    High-confidence duplicate works are combined before analysis using DOI or conservative title-and-author matching. Combined records show all recorded years and retain original IDs and DOIs. Unresolved IDs remain separate. Each source counts once, even when its reference list contains multiple versions. Similar titles alone are insufficient to merge different works.

    Closed bundles represent supported subsets within the selected reference universe. Source topical relevance still requires researcher review. Previously saved findings retain their historical analysis; rebuild from the search to apply the current cleanup.
