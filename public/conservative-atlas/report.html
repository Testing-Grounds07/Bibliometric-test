Every supported N-item set is contained in a closed set with the same supporting articles. No pairwise triangle inference or itemset truncation is used.

For selected N, the chart projects qualifying bundles onto cited-work pairs. Edge support is the deduplicated union of qualifying bundle-supporting articles. Closed bundles and supporting evidence are also stored natively in Neo4j.

  • Source metadata eligibility is not topical relevance or verified peer review.

  • Closed frequent sets losslessly represent supported subsets; displayed bundles may contain more than the selected N.

  • CPM resolutions 0.02 and 0.1 use ten starts each; substantive validity and bootstrap stability are not established.
  • Distinct OpenAlex IDs are not automatically merged.

Unified all-orders view: experimental combined score

S(i,j) = sum over distinct co-citing source articles a of [1 + log2(K(i,j,a) - 1)]

K is the largest qualifying jointly cited bundle containing the pair and supported by this article; K=2 for pair-only evidence. Qualifying bundles have at least five distinct supporting articles. Only one largest-order value is used per article and pair, regardless of the number of overlapping bundles.

A pair-only article contributes 1; an article supporting a triple contributes 2; one supporting a ten-reference bundle contributes about 4.17. Score is weighted evidence, not an article count, probability, significance test, or validated bibliometric standard.

S divided by the product of the two original reference citing-article counts; then mean-edge-weight scaling for Leiden CPM.

Rewards larger jointly cited contexts by design. Does not establish topical specificity or correct all effects of long bibliographies. It depends on reference scope and support threshold. Order-profile rows are nested and must not be added as independent observations.

Line thickness uses S on a compressed scale. The detail panel reports the original article count, bonus, normalized analysis weight, and nested support at every order. Node size remains original citation frequency. This projection is not a direct hypergraph community detector.

Background: random walks and community detection in hypergraphs. Background on projection choices; the logarithmic score here is a custom exploratory choice, not the cited paper’s metric.
