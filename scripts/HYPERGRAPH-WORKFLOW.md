# Whole-group clustering and 3D lab

Only newly selected **Hypergraph** networks under Leiden or Infomap use `incidence-v1`. Pairwise, higher-order pair scoring, and the experimental direct hypergraph modularity optimizer remain unchanged. Historical maps without that version retain their legacy projection interpretation until rebuilt; do not reuse their settings scans or reliability as incidence evidence.

## Representation and algorithms

Exact supported co-citation combinations retain the existing source-normalized group weights. For coupling, each distinct reference defines a group of eligible source articles. One helper node represents each complete group. Every article–group membership receives that group's weight. No article-pair clique is used for the new clustering path. Total incidence mass of a size-m, weight-w group is m×w; larger groups therefore receive more walk volume. The group weights do not imply independent combinations.

- **Leiden:** official `leidenalg==0.10.2` / `igraph==0.11.9` engine. Normalize incidence weights to mean one. Use `CPMVertexPartition.Bipartite` with cross-class gamma and zero within-class resolutions, then optimize its three multiplex layers with weights `[1,-1,-1]` until convergence, retaining the best of ten seeded starts. Gamma is on a new scale. The default starting point is incidence density / 4, not an Auto recommendation. Find settings scans that scale and bootstraps the original evidence using this same engine.
- **Infomap:** official browser engine, two-level undirected weighted incidence flow. Both article and helper-group visits are encoded. The walk can return to its originating article. This is deliberately not the article-only bipartite projection and not a multilayer/state-memory walk. Group visits affect the objective. It preserves incidence information but does not guarantee substantively different or better communities. Markov-time settings scans and reliability use the identical representation.
- Return and canonicalize article memberships only. Helper-only modules are not counted. Pair lines, connected-cluster filters and bridge roles still use original pair evidence; these are not the incidence optimizer's edges. Envelopes retain complete groups.

Direct modularity uses a separate explicit worker dispatch. Only Leiden/Infomap requests prepare incidence; legacy worker requests without an algorithm retain the direct-modularity default. Invalid worker responses reject the pending calculation instead of leaving the canvas stuck at reconstruction. Regression tests cover both the published worker and its browser caller, for co-citation and coupling constructions.

Verified 18 October 2026: 82 community, hypergraph, reliability, settings, shared-canvas and exploded-view regression tests passed. Browser checks on the published co-citation corpus confirmed completed direct-modularity clustering, a functioning 3D article rail, moving projected leaders after camera changes, and real supporting-source records. The original 2D inspector opened separately and returned to 3D unchanged; its published script and stylesheet were not modified. A clean browser run captured no analysis errors. The 3D reset camera now fits complete sheets rather than clipping taller stacks.

The service listens only on localhost:8095, accepts bounded JSON, fits in a subprocess with a 90-second deadline, and rejects oversize inputs without truncation. No fallback silently changes the objective. Browser cancellation stops the client; an already-running server fit can continue until completion/deadline. A busy client retries for up to 90 seconds, abortably, so switching maps does not immediately fail behind a cancelled server fit. Start with `scripts/start-incidence.ps1 -Python <python3-path>`; first run installs pinned dependencies into the ignored `.trials/hypergraph-engine` virtual environment. Serve through the explicit `/conservative-atlas/analysis-api/incidence` proxy route. No credentials are forwarded.

## Automatic settings and stability

New incidence maps and direct-modularity maps in the deployed legacy workspace now queue an automatic settings search once per map, while retaining the current canvas. The compact **Find settings** section and canvas chip show progress and cancellation. Completed current-version audits and explicitly applied settings are not automatically rerun; display changes do not submit scans. Historical legacy projections are not silently upgraded. **Rerun Auto settings** repeats the stability-driven scan; individual candidates remain manually applicable. Existing saved-map snapshots/exports retain the settings audit and applied parameters. This does not activate the separate multiuser rollout: server-backed maps retain explicit scan initiation to avoid repeatedly enqueueing new result IDs, and read-only members cannot initiate scans or apply settings.

Leiden incidence tests a density-scaled half-octave CPM grid (density/32 through density×8, plus the current setting). Infomap tests Markov time 0.25–16: larger times tend to merge flow communities. Direct modularity holds the selected majority/linear/quadratic/strict objective fixed and tests an experimental resolution multiplier 0.25–4. Its score is observed reward minus resolution times expected reward; 1 is exactly the previous score. This is an explicit application-level extension, not a claim to implement the AON blockmodel estimator or h-Louvain. **Compare experimental objectives** remains separate: it evaluates definitions without declaring an automatic winner. Raw objective values cannot be compared across resolutions or definitions.

Each setting uses ten full-data starts, three independent one-start comparisons, and ten conditional evidence bootstraps. A sampled plateau needs at least three eligible settings spanning twofold and every-pair ARI ≥0.90. Eligible coarse/fine representatives receive 50 fresh bootstraps (seed offset 1000); coupling also receives ten 90%-source checks. Auto installs only a fresh-validated representative; if none passes, the map stays exploratory and explicitly says there is no clear recommendation. The overview safeguards require recovery ≥0.70, retention ≥0.80, coverage ≥0.50, start agreement ≥0.80, introduced tiny-node share ≤0.20, and largest-group share ≤0.65. The dominance limit is a declared overview preference, not a theorem. Conditional evidence/candidate universes remain fixed; this is not CHAMP, external validation, or proof of an optimum. Settings-search format is version 2; server fingerprints include the effective direct resolution and objective-comparison choice.

Research basis: [Leiden resolution profiles](https://leidenalg.readthedocs.io/en/stable/advanced.html#resolution-profile), [Infomap resolution options](https://mapequation.org/infomap-python-docs/examples/options-guide.html), [hypergraph modularity definitions](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224307), and [resolution flexibility in AON hypergraph modularity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09611). These motivate method-specific scans, not a universal scientifically correct parameter. Our bootstrap/plateau safeguards are explicitly heuristic.

## All-clusters 3D experiment

For the nontechnical comparison, open `conservative-atlas/hypergraph-help.html` or More → Resources → Clustering explained · animated. It uses the same eight fictional articles and five combinations for eight steps per method. Leiden moves an article, compares connection rewards with group-size cost, regroups temporary units, and offers a CPM detail example. Infomap traces legal incidence routes, counts visits and boundary crossings, and compares stationary-flow description lengths. Direct modularity traces whole envelopes, counts member votes, demonstrates competing combinations, and compares observed togetherness with its expected baseline. Pause, previous/next, step jumping, restart, keyboard tabs, a boundary-article split comparison, a narrow-screen layout and reduced-motion defaults are included. The small numerical comparisons evaluate only the displayed candidates: this is an illustrative teaching page, not a full optimizer execution or a view of project data. The older `methods-explained.html` remains available for pairwise examples and links back to the new comparison.

The main canvas's **3D** renderer uses the exploded inspector's visual language: rounded neutral sheets with soft shadows, flat cluster-colored discs with dark outlines, restrained group fills, readable combination captions and dotted identity guides. It starts exploded with the first combination in focus and a right-hand article rail. The rail lists exact members, tracked context separately when needed, and expandable supporting-source evidence. Dotted leaders and matching numbers project the focused layer's nodes into the article list and follow orbit, zoom, pan, animation and rail scrolling. Hover/focus emphasizes one article without removing other leaders. On narrow screens the rail stacks below the scene. The combination selector, sheet clicks and previous/next controls change the focused layer without restarting the explosion. **Open 2D exploded view** remains a separate action using the original unchanged inspector.

The main renderer anchors a separate stack at each cluster’s existing main-map location, including a base sheet for clusters without complete matching groups. One uniform transform of the full map’s X/Y coordinates preserves cluster arrangement, relative spacing, article positions and relative cluster sizes; no grid, independent cluster resizing, collision relaxation or new layout is introduced. Sheets fit each cluster’s own bounds, and combinations add only vertical depth. Display filters and layer navigation do not change the transform. Historical grid-camera positions are refitted once; new camera records include the layout version. Each stack contains exactly the complete 3+-member combinations that survive filtering to that cluster alone. Cross-cluster groups are not cut into partial combinations or repeated as new evidence; their omission from these stacks is counted explicitly, and they remain available in the 2D network. The independent experiment below retains its different, whole-crossing-group preview policy.

The 3D sheets and their soft shadows follow a rounded, padded convex hull of the cluster’s article positions instead of rectangular bounds. Every layer in a cluster uses the same footprint so identity guides and tracked context stay aligned. Single-node and collinear clusters have circular/capsule outlines. Combination captions appear only on the globally selected layer, following click, scroll and selector navigation; cluster headings remain visible. These contours are visual containers, not additional relationships, and do not move nodes or change membership. The original 2D exploded inspector remains unchanged.

Wheel over a stack to advance or retreat through that cluster’s combinations. Its centered layer and article rail update together, with a smooth vertical translation; the other clusters retain their own layer positions. Wheel elsewhere zooms; Ctrl/Command-wheel and pinch retain zoom. Cluster/combination selectors, previous/next, and PageUp/PageDown/Home/End offer keyboard alternatives. Frame cluster fits one stack, while Reset 3D camera fits the whole overview. Layer position, active cluster, tracking and camera are included in saved view state.

Each cluster previews 4, 8 or 16 layers, with at most 240 members per group and 1,600 occurrences per cluster. A round-robin global budget of 256 sheets and 12,000 member occurrences preserves complete groups and distributes coverage; counts and limits are shown in the status. These are rendering limits, not analytical filters. Empty stacks are retained rather than suggesting nonexistent combinations. Focus dims other sheets within that stack; tracking preserves article identity and the right rail keeps all member leaders. The scene follows light/night tokens, collapse/explode animation and reduced-motion preferences. The original 2D inspector is unchanged.

Verification: 93 targeted regression tests passed, including rounded hull containment and degenerate cases, exact preservation of every pair’s main-map X/Y displacement under one shared scale, invariant positions after display/layer filters, irregular sheet camera fitting, filtered-cluster equivalence, independent layer cursors, wheel accumulation/clamping, complete-group rendering budgets and existing community/reliability/exploded workflows. Browser checks confirmed independent wheel navigation on the shaped sheets, and the published adaptive-leadership map retained its 2D arrangement while updating the focused combination and article leaders without rendering errors.

Open `conservative-atlas/hypergraph-3d-test.html` (also under More → Resources). This independent WebGL/Three.js scene clusters the adaptive leadership study using either new incidence method. It does not modify projects or the main map.

Every linked community has a spatially separated stack, initially exploded, with a floor containing its articles. Raised planes show complete exact groups, not partial within-cluster subsets. A cross-cluster group can repeat in each participating cluster's stack and includes all foreign members in their own colors. These repeated layers are the same evidence, not independent support; do not sum their counts. Clusters with no groups still appear. Each stack previews 1–8 groups, preferring 3+ members then stronger support, with pairs filling remaining slots; the status reports shown/eligible groups. This is an all-clusters preview, **not every hyperedge**. Over 100,000 occurrences fails explicitly. Tracking highlights repeated article identity within and across stacks. Depth, spacing and plane outlines are illustrative, not effect sizes or citation strength. Orbit, pan, zoom, fit, cluster focus, article selection, collapse/explode and reduced-motion support are included.
