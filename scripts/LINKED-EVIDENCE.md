## Method-specific Auto settings

The shared canvas has a collapsed Auto settings panel beside the method selector. New hypergraph incidence/direct maps automatically queue a stability-driven scan, retaining the current map while computing. Only a fresh-validated representative from a stable tested range is installed; otherwise the map stays explicitly exploratory. Rerun, cancellation, manual candidate application, and JSON export remain supported. Explicitly applied settings and completed current-version audits are not automatically rescanned. Ordinary pair-network scans remain user-initiated. Audits remain attached to their map during tab switches; save a map/export to retain the legacy workspace audit across reloads. See [hypergraph Auto workflow](HYPERGRAPH-WORKFLOW.md#automatic-settings-and-stability).

- Leiden scans a density-scaled CPM grid on the fixed analytical network.
- Infomap scans Markov times 0.25–16, retaining two-level undirected flow. Each applied Markov time is also used by reliability resamples.
- Direct hypergraph holds the chosen objective fixed and sweeps an experimental expected-baseline multiplier (0.25–4). A value of 1 preserves the previous score. The separate majority/linear/quadratic/strict objective comparison never declares an automatic winner.
- Every candidate uses ten full-data starts, three independent one-start checks, and ten common screening resamples. Resolution ranges require at least three consecutive tested points spanning twofold, every-pair ARI ≥0.90, recovery ≥0.70, retention ≥0.80, coverage ≥0.50, start agreement ≥0.80, introduced tiny-node share ≤0.20 and largest-cluster share ≤0.65. The latter is an overview preference, not a theoretical cutoff. Intact disconnected components are exempt from tiny-fragment penalties, but partitions consisting only of disconnected components cannot be recommended as subdivisions.
- Eligible broad/fine representatives get 50 fresh resamples at seed offset 1000. All explicit objective-comparison alternatives get these checks. Validation failures can remove a recommendation. Fresh draws are not independent external validation; heuristics express overview preferences, not correctness probabilities or significance.
- Coupling additionally supports ten 90%-source subsamples at offset 2000. Sources are sampled without replacement; reference lists remain intact. Removed sources count as losses, making this a conservative sensitivity measure, not directly comparable with the reference-feature bootstrap. Finalists need recovery ≥0.60 and retention ≥0.70 on this complementary check. Standalone source checks are available even without an Auto recommendation.
- Evidence sensitivity is separate and never auto-applies: stricter coupling thresholds; higher co-citation support; and pairwise-only cap for combined-order views. These scans use the existing node/candidate universe, not new mining. Single-order comparisons remain available through the canvas order selector. Raw quality values are never compared across objectives or resolutions.

Implementation: `method-settings.mjs`, `method-settings-worker.mjs`, `canvas-settings.mjs`, plus the shared community engine and reliability worker. Build all changed browser bundles with esbuild, including `method-settings-worker.js`, before deployment.

Research basis: [CPM resolution plateaus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3796307/), [Infomap Markov time](https://mapequation.org/infomap-python-docs/examples/options-guide.html), [hypergraph objective families](https://academic.oup.com/comnet/article/12/5/cnae041/7829128), and [citation resampling dependencies](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0053943). This implementation is not CHAMP and does not implement null-model significance testing.

The Community method selector is shared by both map tabs. Each tab remembers its own method and canvas state. Coupling now offers Leiden CPM, Infomap (cosine-weighted pair network), and direct hypergraph modularity (one unit-weight hyperedge per distinct shared reference among threshold-eligible connected source articles). Duplicate references within a source are counted once. Pair thresholds choose the eligible source universe; whole reference groups can connect sources whose individual pair fails the threshold. ## Method-specific reliability

`network-reliability.mjs` prepares deterministic 10-resample preliminary or 50-resample fuller checks. Every resample reruns the chosen method, with two optimization starts (Leiden CPM, Infomap map equation at Markov time 1, or direct hypergraph modularity). Co-citation resamples source articles and reweights fixed pair/hyperedge candidates, reapplies joint-support thresholds, and recomputes source-normalized hyperedge weights; it does not mine new combinations. Coupling resamples reference IDs, rebuilds cosine thresholds and eligible source membership, and assigns each retained reference hyperedge its sampled multiplicity. Sources are fixed. Optimization variation is included. Percentile spreads are not confidence intervals or validation of themes.

`reliability-worker.mjs` performs preparation, Leiden and hypergraph computations off the UI thread; `community-browser.mjs` delegates Infomap samples to the official engine. Cancellation terminates workers/engine; incomplete checks never appear as completed results. The inline panel caches results by exact graph object, cancels when changing analysis or tabs, follows cluster filters for presentation only, and provides plain-English recovery, retention and pair-network bridge roles. Exports include the completed audit. Changing analytical settings creates a new result scope; results are not automatically persisted to Neo4j.

Build the additional worker with `npx --yes esbuild@0.25.12 scripts/reliability-worker.mjs --bundle --format=iife --outfile=public/conservative-atlas/reliability-worker.js` and publish it alongside the updated community and evidence bundles.

## Data and coupling

Run `node scripts/build-source-evidence.mjs` against the existing downloaded works. The builder requires every original conservative source ID, writes only scholarly metadata and reference incidence, and records an incidence SHA-256. It makes no API calls. The initial dataset contains 2,855 sources and 112,855 distinct referenced OpenAlex IDs, with no empty reference lists. Available reference lists are not claimed complete. Unavailable reference titles display IDs; different OpenAlex IDs are never merged.

Coupling counts distinct shared references and uses cosine normalization: shared count divided by the square root of the two available bibliography lengths. Default analytical thresholds are three shared references and cosine ≥0.05. Every available reference participates, including references below the co-citation citation-frequency cutoff. Sources with empty lists remain explicitly distinguishable from sources with no eligible links.

Ten seeded Leiden CPM starts use mean-edge-weight scaling from the existing partition engine. A blank resolution chooses scaled graph density / 4 as an explicitly exploratory starting point, not an Auto CPM stability recommendation. Manual resolutions and both thresholds are exposed. Display filtering does not recluster. The chart renders all matching source nodes, with a strongest-three-per-node union edge filter; the complete analytical graph remains in the export. The source list is paginated at 40; all sources remain accessible. Coordinates pack clusters for readability and are not similarity distances. Mouse pan/zoom, keyboard node/edge activation and touch pan/pinch/tap/long-press inspection are supported.

## Coupling settings comparison

**Compare coupling settings** evaluates shared-reference/cosine threshold pairs (1, 0.02), (3, 0.05), (5, 0.10), plus the current pair, with four scaled-density CPM settings and the current manual CPM where supplied. Each candidate uses ten baseline Leiden starts and eight seeded reference-ID bootstrap samples with two starts each. Selection balances coverage of all scoped sources, member-weighted Jaccard recovery and introduced tiny-group fragmentation. Coverage cannot be improved merely by hiding sources. Safeguards require coverage ≥60%, recovery ≥70%, retention ≥80%, introduced tiny-member share ≤20%, an internal subdivision beyond disconnected components, and no group holding over 90% of linked sources. These cutoffs are explicit exploratory preferences, not established optimal values.

Among candidates passing safeguards, choose fewer groups within 0.03 of the best score (coverage × recovery − 0.20 × introduced tiny-member fraction). If none pass, label the selected near-best trade-off exploratory. Check that candidate on 50 additional reference bootstrap samples, seed offsets 100–149, rather than reusing the eight screening samples. A promising recommendation requires both screening and additional checks to pass. Otherwise display **No clear best settings**. Nothing changes until the user applies a candidate. Applying the validated candidate retains its recovery results; other rows require their own full reliability check. The complete grid, reasons, formula and scores are downloadable. This bounded comparison neither establishes a stable resolution interval nor guarantees the best possible setting. Reference-list resampling does not test corpus selection or missing evidence.

The distinction between network construction and clustering follows the [bibliometrix methodology](https://www.iris.unina.it/retrieve/e268a732-2ef6-4c8f-e053-1705fe0a812c/1-s2.0-S1751157717300500-main.pdf); CPM resolution exploration is described in the [Leiden documentation](https://leidenalg.readthedocs.io/en/stable/advanced.html). The scoring and recommendation rules here are BiblioMap heuristics, not claims from those sources.

## Article roles

Weighted participation is `1 - sum((strength to cluster / total strength)^2)`. External share is the fraction of total normalized weight reaching another cluster. Within-cluster z uses within-cluster weighted strength and population standard deviation; zero variance produces N/A rather than a fabricated zero. Unlinked nodes have no defined participation. Cross-cluster connector is descriptive for any nonzero external weight; it is not a centrality threshold, betweenness claim, relevance decision or disciplinary interpretation. Generic methodological references can be connectors. Reference roles appear in the main node inspector and the explorer; source roles appear in coupling details. All analytical edges are used, regardless of display filters. The reference ranking displays 100 entries; the export includes every role.

## Cluster recovery

Stability is explicitly requested in the inline canvas panel to avoid blocking mobile interaction. The worker runs 10 or 50 deterministic bootstrap samples with replacement (seed 20261016 + sample), two starts of the selected algorithm per sample; CPM is held fixed only for Leiden. For each baseline cluster it finds the best Jaccard match to a resampled cluster, independent of label numbering. Missing members count against recovery; splits and merges reduce overlap. Mean linked-member retention is reported separately. The 10th–90th percentile is an empirical spread, not a confidence interval. Tiny intact components and trivial partitions may be stable without being substantively useful. Unlinked nodes are excluded from cluster recovery.

Co-citation reuses the existing conditional source-article bootstrap, with the current complete analytical graph and correct higher-order bundles. Reference membership, initial eligible edges, mined bundles and drill-neighbor selection are held fixed. Existing Auto CPM choices and saved memberships are unchanged.

Coupling instead bootstraps distinct available reference IDs, weighting each incidence by its multiplicity, and recalculates cosine normalization and analytical thresholds. All source pairs with any shared reference are reconsidered, including those initially below threshold. The selected source set is fixed. This is reference-list sensitivity, not the same uncertainty model as source-article resampling. It does not test corpus selection or missing metadata. Resampling results are not evidence of thematic correctness.

Plain-English outcomes precede the technical table: **Mostly holds together**, **Sensitive to the evidence**, or **Changes substantially**. The interpretation considers both recovery and retention and flags very small groups or a single-group partition. The overview counts articles as well as clusters, so many stable pairs cannot mask a large unstable cluster. Numerical scores, percentile spreads and exact heuristic cutoffs remain under a disclosure. These labels are not confidence levels, probabilities of correctness, or validation of thematic relevance.

## Builds, exports and deployment

Build the two browser bundles with:

```
npx --yes esbuild@0.25.12 scripts/evidence-worker.mjs --bundle --format=iife --outfile=public/conservative-atlas/evidence-worker.js
npx --yes esbuild@0.25.12 scripts/evidence-explorer.mjs --bundle --format=iife --outfile=public/conservative-atlas/evidence-explorer.js
node --test scripts/evidence-metrics.test.mjs scripts/shared-canvas.test.mjs
```

Exports include the parent memberships and settings, incidence hash, full coupling nodes and edges, roles, computed recovery, settings-comparison audit, interpretation rules, display filters and limitations. Exploratory results are not automatically written to Neo4j; use the JSON export to retain a run. Switching workspace tabs cancels a running worker, but preserves completed results for the same analysis. Rebuilding coupling clears its old stability results unless applying the independently checked comparison candidate; changing the parent clears incompatible results. No existing database records are rewritten.

Publish `source-evidence.json`, `evidence-worker.js`, `evidence-explorer.js`, `evidence-explorer.css`, `app.js` and `index.html` to the existing static host. Do not expose the database or API. Statistical edge validation, block models and temporal analysis are not part of this milestone.
